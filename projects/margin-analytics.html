04 PROJECT CASE STUDY

Product-line margin intelligence.

Connecting inventory, product costing, and sales data to the profitability questions leadership needs answered.

FOCUS
Financial analysis, executive reporting
TOOLS / DISCIPLINES
Acumatica Generic Inquiries · Excel · Cost accounting
PERIOD
2024–2026

The business question

When costs, components, and pricing evolve, leadership needs to understand which products and product lines are generating healthy contribution—and where targeted action might help.

My contribution

I prepared product-line margin analyses directly for the CEO and CFO and supported inventory valuation, item costing, pricing uploads, and related reporting. I also worked with engineering on BOM structures that influence product cost accuracy.

The analytical framework

  1. Establish the relevant product and reporting population.
  2. Confirm cost definitions, time period, and units of measure.
  3. Reconcile or investigate anomalous cost and margin results.
  4. Group results into actionable product-line views.
  5. Explain assumptions, exceptions, and limitations to decision-makers.

Why it matters

A margin report is useful only if leadership can trust the underlying item data and understand the drivers. This work connects detailed accounting controls to higher-level pricing and operating decisions.
